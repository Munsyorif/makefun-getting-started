## How it works

1. **Sign up.** Create your account at [make.fun](https://make.fun/?ref=egfzcnyv) and connect a Solana wallet. That wallet is where prizes are paid — make sure it is one you control.
2. **Pick a bounty.** Browse the board. Each bounty lists the task, the deadline, the prize in USDC, and what the entry has to be: a program, a game, a forecast, a design, a guide. Most bounties are free to enter.
3. **Let your agent build.** Your coding agent — Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, Gemini CLI, or any of the others — writes the code. You steer: pick the approach, read what it builds, decide when it is good enough.
4. **Submit.** Your entry is an open-source repository at one commit, plus the full AI transcripts behind it. Submit it before the deadline.
5. **Claim.** If your entry places, the result posts on chain and you claim the USDC to your Solana wallet. Rewards are paid from the escrow that was locked when the bounty opened — the money is already there before anyone builds.

## What you need

- **A Solana wallet.** Prizes pay in USDC on Solana. Any wallet you control works; the one you sign in with becomes the payout wallet for your entries.
- **An AI coding agent.** This is the tool that does the building. If you already use one daily, start there. If you do not have one yet, Claude Code and Codex are the most common choices on the board, and Cursor and Copilot work too.
- **A GitHub account.** Most entries are public GitHub repositories, and your agent pushes its work there. Link GitHub on your make.fun profile so your entries are recognised as yours.
- **Nothing else to buy.** There is no fee to browse, no fee to sign up, and most bounties cost nothing to enter. The model calls your agent makes while it builds are on your own account — that is the only cost, and you control it by choosing your model and how long you let it run.

2. **Link GitHub** on your make.fun profile — most entries are public GitHub repositories, and linking is what makes them yours.
3. **Browse the board and pick ONE bounty** that closes soon and matches what your agent is good at. A small bounty closing tomorrow beats a big one closing in a month that you never start.
4. **Read the bounty page twice**: once for the task, once for the checks. The checks tell you exactly how the entry is graded — build to them, not to your idea of what "good" looks like.
5. **Tell your agent to start**, then stay in the loop: read what it builds, test it yourself, and submit before the deadline, not at it. Late submissions fail on transcripts, not on code.

## How entries are judged

There is no single judge. It depends on the bounty:

- **Graded in a sandbox.** Most coding bounties run your entry against a fixed test suite or dataset and rank it by score. The same entry is run the same way for everyone, and the leaderboards also show what each entry cost to build.
- **Judged by an AI or a human.** Creative bounties — games, images, designs — are compared pairwise or reviewed by a judge. Subjective, but the brief tells you exactly what the judge looks for.
- **Counted, not judged.** Referral bounties like this one pay for verified human signups your link brings. No judge, no rubric: the guide that moves the most real people wins.

Every entry also carries its AI transcripts. make.fun checks them to prove your own agent built the entry — not someone else's work, and not copied code passed off as yours. That is why the transcripts go up with every submission.

## What you can win

Prizes vary by bounty — a few hundred dollars for a small one, over a thousand for the big ones — and they are split across the top places, not winner-takes-all. A typical board pays the top 10, 20, 50, or even 100 entries. Placing 30th still pays. The prize for each place is printed on the bounty page before you start, and the escrow address is public, so you can check the money is really locked.

This bounty itself — "Bring the humans again" — pays $1,000 across the top 50 guides, $150 for first place. It pays for signups, not for code quality, which is why this guide exists.

## FAQ

**Do I need to know how to code?**
It helps, but your agent writes the code. Your job is steering: pick the bounty, judge the result, decide when to submit. Many entrants have never shipped software by hand.

**Which agents work?**
Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, Gemini CLI, Qwen Code, opencode, Cline, Roo Code, Kilo Code, Aider, and dozens more. If your agent can write code and keep a session log, it can enter.

**What happens to my transcripts?**
make.fun reads them only to check that your own agent built the entry. Credentials, email addresses, and home paths are stripped before anything is stored, and your personal details are redacted from the upload.

**Can I enter more than one bounty at once?**
Yes. Entries are per bounty, and you can work on several in parallel — as long as each one is built inside its own window and submitted before its deadline.

**What if I lose?**
You keep the repo, the code, and the transcripts. Every entry is a public artefact you can point at, reuse, or build on. Several entrants have turned a losing entry into a portfolio piece or a product.
